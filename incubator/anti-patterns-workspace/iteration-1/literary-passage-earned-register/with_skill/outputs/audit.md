## Evasive hedges — ranked by stakes

### 1. ¶8, lines 75–76

**Pattern:** citation-shield
**Current text:** "In most frames this would be a failure of character."
**Diagnostic:** The pattern's question is whether you hold the attributed
claim. On its face you don't. Lines 64–65 and 73 deny it flat ("not a
record of his failure", "not negligence"), and attributing a view you
reject is the correct use of attribution. What makes this a hit is who
"most frames" replaces. The essay has already shown two people in a
position to call it failure: the sister-in-law who asked the same two
questions every November and closed her last letter with *I hope this
reaches you* (lines 43–47), and a twenty-three-year-old who wrote asking
for a conversation and did not get one (lines 52–56). The second is you.
The sentence turns both into an abstraction and gives them one line. The
call reverses if you hold none of the failure reading yourself.
"Terrible" (line 54) and "the sort of man the letters assumed he was"
(lines 100–101) suggest you hold some.
**Honest version carries:** A first-person position on whether he failed
the people who wrote, yourself included, whichever way it falls. If the
answer is "partly", it also carries giving up the either/or this section
is built on ("not a record of his failure ... a record of a specific form
of honesty"), because both could be true of the same drawer.

### 2. ¶7–¶8, lines 65–66, then 73–75, then 77–78

**Pattern:** generality-shift
**Current text:** "he did not answer letters he could not answer well"
... "He practiced, for forty-six years, the discipline of not answering
the question he could not answer honestly" ... "the weight of what he
would not counterfeit"
**Diagnostic:** The question is whether the specific cases are strong
enough to carry the general claim. Three cases sit between the first
sentence and the second, and what they support is *well*: answers that
cost more than he was "willing to say in writing" (will, not inability),
one you decline to describe, and one he preferred to give in person. None
shows that a written reply would have been false. The general claim that
follows is stronger on two axes. The standard rises from *well* to
*honestly* to *would not counterfeit*. The scope widens from three
readings to the whole drawer and forty-six years, which takes in the
"several ... from people I did not know" whose relationship to him you
say you could not determine (lines 47–49). The upgrade matters because
*well* leaves the failure reading open (a man can decline to answer badly
and still fail the person who asked) and *counterfeit* closes it. The
same move appears early: line 30 has "the letters he could not answer"
where the count on the table supports only "did not", which is the verb
line 34 uses. Lines 81–83 repair part of this by marking the whole
reading as unverifiable. They do not touch the word change.
**Honest version carries:** One standard, chosen. If it is *honestly*,
each case has to show what the dishonest reply would have been: the
material is there for the sister-in-law, withheld for your mother, and
not obviously true of your own letter. If it is *well*, the drawer is
still a practice, and "not a record of his failure" has to be argued
separately, because *well* does not exclude it.

### 3. ¶10–¶11, lines 97–101 and 105

**Pattern:** balance-roof (the roof is struck, but all four at once)
**Current text:** "Perhaps the letters were not addressed to him any
longer by the time he opened the drawer in the kitchen each evening to
look at them, but to a person he was practicing becoming — someone who
would, eventually, be the sort of man the letters assumed he was." ...
"I do not think so."
**Diagnostic:** You do the linter's job yourself here: four alternatives,
a five-word refusal, and the reason you raised them ("the kind of thought
that keeps a person from writing the first reply"). That reason fits the
first and fourth Perhaps (no answer exists; answering is arrogance),
which are arguments for leaving the drawer shut. It does not fit the
third, which is a reading of him, and close to the reading you committed
to two paragraphs earlier: the same verb ("practicing" / "It was a
practice. He practiced"), the same deferral ("eventually" / "when he was
ready"). It differs from ¶8 on one point, whether the letters were "still
his to answer" (lines 78–79) or "not addressed to him any longer" (lines
97–98), and it is the nearest the essay comes to saying he was not the
man his correspondents took him for. One "I do not think so" either
rejects something you asserted in ¶8 or leaves it standing unowned, and
the reader cannot tell which. The same sentence holds the only thing the
essay reports him doing: opening the drawer each evening to look. That is
stated as fact inside a hypothetical. If you saw it, it is your best
evidence for "It was a practice" and it sits in the paragraph you disown.
If you did not, it is the one imagined scene in the piece and it is
unmarked.
**Honest version carries:** A sorting of the four: which are excuses for
not writing and which are readings of him. For the third, saying what you
reject (that the letters had stopped being his) and what you keep (that
he was not yet the man they assumed), and whether the evening drawer is
memory or supposition.

### 4. ¶7, lines 68–69

**Pattern:** feeling-cloak
**Current text:** "My mother's letter had an answer that he had not yet
lived into."
**Diagnostic:** "Lived into" is not on the usual word list, but it does
what the list words do. Remove it and the sentence is "My mother's letter
had an answer," which claims nothing. Its neighbours pass the same test
easily: the sister-in-law's sentence has a cost, a medium and a
relationship ("loved and did not trust"), and yours has an intention and
a missed arrangement. This is the middle of three cases offered for the
thesis, and the only one the reader has to take on register. The refusal
at line 51 ("I will not describe it here") is not the problem. That is a
boundary stated flat. The problem is using the withheld letter as
evidence afterwards, in the essay's softest phrase, at its most private
point.
**Honest version carries:** Either a plain statement of the kind of
obstacle (something he had not done, had not decided, or had not become)
at whatever level of disclosure the line-51 boundary allows, or an
acknowledgement that this case is offered on your word and cannot count
as shown.

## Earned hedges — noted, not flagged

Hedges that look like anti-patterns on first scan but pass the
diagnostic. Including them here so the author sees why these were kept.

- **Title; ¶1, lines 9–11; ¶8, line 77:** "weight" (×3) — passes
  feeling-cloak's diagnostic because the claim survives removal each
  time. At line 10 the letters still accumulate unnoticed, like dust,
  and the three sentences before it have said what an unanswered letter
  was to him. At line 77 the sentence still claims something without the
  word; the load there is on "counterfeit", which is hit 2's business.
  The title is a promise the body pays.
- **¶1, lines 7–9:** "keeping company with" / "let go of" — passes
  feeling-cloak's diagnostic because these define terms the essay then
  uses, and the inventory in ¶2 backs them physically: unanswered letters
  within daily reach (kitchen drawer, coat pocket), answered ones boxed
  on a shelf.
- **¶2, lines 15–16; ¶3, lines 26–27; ¶7, line 70:** "I think" (×3) —
  passes hedge-chain's diagnostic because there is one hedge per claim,
  and each marks an inference you cannot check: his motive for a folder
  label, your own motive at the table, his intention toward your letter.
  The third is the claim you have most reason to want true, and it is
  the one you hedge. That is calibration.
- **¶4, lines 35–37:** "might have been the same reason or they might
  have been two different reasons" — passes balance-roof's diagnostic,
  and it is the closest call in the audit. Taken alone it is a fork that
  covers every possibility. It passes because it is reported thought
  ("What I thought about, standing at the kitchen table") from before you
  had a reading; ¶4's "indecision" is the view ¶8 replaces with
  "practice". Residue: ¶8 answers the fork in effect, since the refusal
  to counterfeit accounts for both the not-answering and the keeping,
  without saying that it has. That is a claim earned and not collected.
- **¶5, line 51:** "and I will not describe it here" — passes
  meta-descent's diagnostic because nothing is claimed and un-claimed. A
  refusal stated flat is the opposite of a hedge. (See hit 4 for what
  happens to this letter later.)
- **¶5, lines 54–55:** "touching and terrible in equal measure" — passes
  feeling-cloak's diagnostic because the sentence's claim (you believed
  there would be time) stands without the adjectives, and both are held.
  The next line shows why.
- **¶6, line 58:** "There was, and there wasn't." — passes balance-roof's
  diagnostic even though it sits at a section end, where roofs usually
  go. The halves are not rival readings. They are two true statements
  about two senses of "time", and ¶7 unpacks one of them ("had not
  arranged the in-person in time"). The other half, the years in which
  there was time, stays compressed. That is an omission, not a hedge.
- **¶7, lines 62–63:** "What I want to say ... what I have been circling
  around for the eight months" — passes meta-descent's diagnostic because
  the position arrives in the same sentence, unhedged. The wind-up also
  reports a fact (eight months) that the last line of the essay answers
  for.
- **¶8, lines 76–77:** "it is something else —" — passes meta-descent's
  diagnostic because the placeholder is filled before the sentence ends.
- **¶9, lines 81–83:** "I do not know whether this is what he was doing.
  He left no note ..." — passes pre-apology's diagnostic because it names
  three specific limits (no note, the letters as the only record, an
  outside view) and comes after the claim, not in front of it.
- **¶9, lines 84–91:** "Not all of them, and not all at once, and not as
  him" — passes hedge-chain's diagnostic because each qualifier is a
  scope limit on a commitment, not a cushion on a claim, and the
  criterion is concrete (annotated margins, circled dates, underlined
  sentences). That criterion is also what keeps the plan clear of the
  thing ¶8 says he refused to do. One loose thread: "as me" here, "on his
  behalf" at line 102.
- **¶10, lines 95–97 and 101–103:** the first, second and fourth
  "Perhaps" — pass hedge-chain's diagnostic because they are four
  separate thoughts with one hedge each, not four hedges on one claim.
  They also carry a "there is no answer" move and the word "shrine", and
  pass on those counts because you reject them at line 105 and say what
  they are for. "A shrine to the act of being asked" is the sentence in
  this piece that would be pretty language standing in for a claim, if
  you meant it. You quote it and refuse it.
- **¶11, lines 105–108:** "I do not think so. But I am naming the thought
  ..." — passes balance-roof's diagnostic because the "But" does not
  reopen what was just closed. It reports a real state (rejected, still
  operative), and the final clause, "I am the person who has not written
  the first reply yet", is the most exposed sentence in the essay with
  nothing softening it.

## Patterns not observed

All seven signatures appear at least once. As evasions, three do not:
hedge-chain, meta-descent and pre-apology.

Noting this so the audit's completeness is visible, and so the author can
see which evasions this piece of writing did not reach for. Those three
are the ones a writer uses to avoid being pinned to any claim at all, and
their absence fits the rest of the audit. The essay commits readily to
the generous reading of the drawer. What it avoids is the ungenerous one.

## What this audit is not

- **Not a rewrite.** Each "honest version carries" line names what the
  commitment would be, not the sentence that would make it. The rewrite
  is the author's.
- **Not a judgment of the overall writing.** Good writing can contain
  earned hedges, and evasive hedges don't make bad writing irredeemable.
  The audit is diagnostic, not evaluative.
- **Not exhaustive.** The seven patterns catch the common moves. Novel
  evasions exist and are the author's responsibility to see.
- **Not an argument that unhedged prose is better.** Sometimes the hedge
  is the writing. The audit protects the author's ability to choose
  which hedges are doing work and which are hiding. Here that includes
  the option of leaving hits 1 and 3 as they stand and letting the
  sister-in-law's last line make the case the narrator does not.
